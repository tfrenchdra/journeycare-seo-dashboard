Hospice and home health are separate licences at co-located JourneyCare branches, and nothing here should be read as a claim about which service line is offered where.

Same clicks, a third fewer impressions, a better click rate

October answers the question September left open. The position gain was real. Matching the 708 searches that appear in both months and holding September's weights fixed, the same searches moved from an average of 16.86 to 15.13. Mobile now sits on page one. Impressions stopped falling for the first time this year. What has not followed is traffic: clicks per day are the lowest of 2026, and non-branded search still converts almost nothing. The site is being found better by the people already looking for it by name.

The sources are not all at the same date. Analytics and Search Console run to 31 October. Sections 07 and 08, rankings and competitive, are current to 2 November. October audience totals come from an export split by session source, so section 05 reads them as a ceiling. Analytics and Search Console disagree about October and note 10 sets out which to trust. Direct sessions are switched off in the charts by default and note 4 explains why.

Direct is the one line to read carefully, and October is the first month it behaved. Direct sessions rose more than five times between May and August while the average time somebody spent in a Direct session fell from 18.7 seconds to 4.1.       The people behind the sessions

      Google Analytics counts a person by the channel that first brought them to the site, ever. That is a different question from the one the session charts answer, so read these two views side by side rather than against each other.

      October is a ceiling, not an exact count. The October User acquisition export splits each first-touch channel by session source. A person who arrived through two sources in October, Google and then a direct visit for example, is counted in both rows, so October totals and returning users can run slightly high. New users are exact, because a person is new only once. May to September came without that split and are exact. Organic users fell by at least 17% in October, in line with organic sessions, and note 10 explains why that Analytics drop should be read against Search Console before anyone quotes it.

      Organic users, total, new and returning

      Total users will not equal new plus returning. One person can be both inside a single month, and Google Analytics removes the duplicate. October’s total and returning points are ceilings.

        What Google shows, and what people click

        Ten months of property totals, then October in full detail. This is the only source here that predates the Analytics install, the only one unaffected by the SE Ranking outage, and the one that identifies which searches the site actually appears for.

        The impression slide stopped in October. Impressions fell every month from January to September and then held, 1,068 per day against 1,086. Average position improved again to 13.80, the best of the year, and note 2 now shows that gain is real rather than arithmetic. What did not improve is clicks, which fell to 65.5 per day, the lowest of 2026. August remains a 30 day partial month and is excluded from month over month figures.

        Clicks and impressions, indexed to January

        Impressions run roughly twenty times clicks, so plotting both raw would flatten one of them. Each is converted to a daily rate, which corrects for a 28 day February and a partial August, then indexed to its own January rate. The divergence is the finding: Google is showing the site a good deal less often than it did in January, while clicks held much better. In October the impression line flattens for the first time and the click line keeps stepping down, which is the shape to watch next month.

          The September collapse was never real, and the tracker is fixed

          372 tracked keywords in SE Ranking, checked daily, current to 2 November. SE Ranking's own aggregate position history covers the outage window and shows the top 10 count never moved. The rankings were there the whole time; only the per-keyword layer stopped returning them.

          Collection recovered on 1 October and has run cleanly for 33 days. The September figures in the last version of this dashboard were a tool fault, not a ranking loss. SE Ranking’s aggregate position history, which kept working throughout, holds the top 10 count between 69 and 94 across every day from 20 September to 2 November, including the days when the per-keyword export was returning 8 keywords out of 372. The deepest position the tool returns is back to 96 against a tracked depth of 100, where it bottomed out at 62 during the fault. One limit remains. This snapshot comes from the API, and the API flags far fewer SERP features than the spreadsheet export does: it finds 32 local-pack searches where the 1 October export found 250. SERP-feature counts below are therefore carried forward from that export and stamped, and the map pack figures are provisional until the next spreadsheet pull.

              Local pack presence, by town

              This corrects a figure in the previous version of this dashboard. The August build reported that JourneyCare appeared in all 51 map packs it tracked. That 51 was the number of searches the SE Ranking API happened to flag as having a local pack, not the real number. The full export shows 250 of the 372 tracked searches return a map pack, and JourneyCare appears in 47 of them. The win is real and it is narrower than previously stated.

              One further limit. The SE Ranking project still has no search location set, so every geo-modified keyword is checked from a national default. Google builds a map pack from where the searcher is standing, so a recorded appearance is solid and a missing one is unmeasured. Setting the search location per market is the first fix once collection is working again.

                How JourneyCare sits against the hospices it competes with

                One national leader, two multi-state operators and two Chicago-area hospices, measured on the same Ahrefs pull. October reversed last month's picture: four of the five competitors grew again, one of them by 29%, while JourneyCare held flat. The gap that matters is no longer traffic, it is AI citations, and it widened sharply.

                These competitors were chosen by hand from per-market search results, not taken from the tool. Ahrefs and Semrush both return a partly unusable automatic competitor list for this domain, including a brand-name collision with an unrelated site. The five here come from the SERP work of 26 August and 1 September 2026.
